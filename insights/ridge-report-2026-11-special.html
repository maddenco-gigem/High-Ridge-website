Special Market Commentary

November Special  ·  McKinney, Texas

The S&P 500 just had its worst week in over six years. The Nvidia story has become a national security story. And Thanksgiving is a few days away. Here's the plain-language version of what's happening — and where things go from here.

A Note Before Thanksgiving

Before we talk markets, I want to take a moment.

Thanksgiving is a few days away, and no matter what the market is doing — I hope you'll take time to step back from the noise and be genuinely grateful. For your family. Your health. The people around your table.

Markets go up and markets go down. That's always been true. What doesn't change is what actually matters.

"Shout for joy to the Lord, all the earth. Worship the Lord with gladness; come before him with joyful songs. Know that the Lord is God. From my family to yours — Happy Thanksgiving. I'm grateful for the trust you place in me.

What Happened Last Week

Last week was the worst week for the S&P 500 in over six years. The index fell 8.6% and is now roughly 12% below its October high — officially in correction territory, closing Friday at 6,910. The Nasdaq had its worst week since March 2020. These are not normal weekly moves. (S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite index data, week ending November 21, 2026)

What drove it? A combination of forces arriving at the same time: the Federal Reserve signaling that nearly all policymakers expect more rate hikes, inflation still running above target, and a sudden leadership shock at Nvidia — the company at the center of the entire AI trade. When those forces arrive together, the selling tends to be broad and fast. That's what we saw.

This morning, markets are bouncing. Futures are up 2–3% across the board. A bounce after the worst week in years is not the same as a recovery. We're watching the data carefully before drawing conclusions.

The Nvidia Story — and What It Reveals

Jensen Huang, Nvidia's founder and CEO for over 30 years, resigned on November 12th. The stock lost roughly $1 trillion in market value in the days that followed — even as the company had reported 103% revenue growth. (Bloomberg/FactSet, Nov 12–21, 2026; Nvidia Q3 FY2027 earnings release) This morning, the board voted unanimously to reinstate him, effective immediately. The stock is up over 9% on the news. (Nvidia press release and SEC Form 8-K filing, November 23, 2026)

His return was cleared by senior U.S. government officials — a finding that it poses no national security risk — and an independent committee has been formed to investigate reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. This is no longer just a corporate story. It's a national security story inside the most systemically important company in AI.

Here's what this tells you as an investor: markets price certainty. The business didn't change. The headline did. And the market moved a trillion dollars in response. That concentration of risk in a very small number of companies — and people — doesn't disappear because the CEO came back.

What History Says — Honestly

In every midterm election year since 1950, the S&P 500 has finished higher in the 12 months following the election — every single time, with an average gain of approximately 15%. (LPL Financial Research / Ned Davis Research, historical midterm cycle analysis)

But midterm years also carry the largest average peak-to-trough drawdown of any year in the four-year presidential cycle — nearly 18% on average. And historically, the worst of that selling tends to come later in the year. (Same source)

What History Suggests

We likely go higher from here — eventually. The midterm pattern has held without exception since 1950.

What It Doesn't Say

That we're completely through the turbulence. The average drawdown in a midterm year is nearly 18%, and we may not have seen the bottom yet.

History says we likely go higher from here — eventually. It also says we may not be completely through the turbulence yet. We think it's important you hear both sides.

The Three Things We're Watching Right Now

1 — The Fed's December Meeting

As of this morning, markets are pricing a 96% probability of another rate hike in December. (CME FedWatch Tool, November 23, 2026) The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, PCE, prints this Wednesday. This is the single most important data point between now and year-end.

2 — The Technical Floor

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 6,910 — meaningfully below the long-term moving average most professionals watch as the line between a healthy pullback and something more serious. We're monitoring it closely.

3 — AI Sector Stress

The AI infrastructure buildout was financed on assumptions that revenue would follow spending. That gap is now visible in credit markets and in the broad tech sell-off last week. This isn't a reason to panic — it's a reason to know what you own.

We are watching. We are being proactive. We're not selling into fear and we're not chasing a bounce. We are actively monitoring the signals that matter, staying disciplined, and positioning to be opportunistic when the right moments appear.
